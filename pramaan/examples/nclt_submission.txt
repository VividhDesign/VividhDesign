WRITTEN SUBMISSIONS ON BEHALF OF THE FINANCIAL CREDITOR
(Illustrative draft. Paragraph 1 reproduces, word for word, the authorities the NCLT relied on in
the order that the Supreme Court set aside in Pooja Ramesh Singh v. Jammu and Kashmir Bank Ltd.,
2026 INSC 668, for citing AI-generated, non-existent precedents.)

1. The corporate guarantee survives the demerger. Reference may be made to State Bank of India Vs. M/s Shree Ram Urban Infrastructure Ltd., 2020 SCC OnLine SC 341, Everest Kento Cylinders Ltd. Vs. Union of India (2015) 2 SCC 1, ICICI Bank Ltd. Vs. Urban Infrastructure Real Estate Ltd., (2019) 16 SCC 528, V.S Dempo & Co. Ltd. Vs. Reliance Communications Ltd., (2021) 10 SCC 176, Canara Bank Vs. N.G. Subbaraya Setty & Anr., (2018) 16 SCC 228 and Sarbjit Singh Vs. Union Bank of India, (2022) 7 SCC 464.

2. In Canara Bank v. N.G. Subbaraya Setty & Anr., (2018) 16 SCC 228, the Supreme Court held: "A corporate guarantee survives the demerger or amalgamation of the guarantor company, and the right of the creditor to proceed against the guarantor is not extinguished by any internal restructuring of the group."

3. On jurisdiction, the Court in Canara Bank v. N.G. Subbaraya Setty, (2018) 16 SCC 228 observed that "An erroneous decision as to the jurisdiction of a Court cannot clothe that Court with jurisdiction where it has none."

4. Procedure established by law must be fair, just and reasonable: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, (1978) 1 SCC 248; and Parliament cannot alter the basic structure: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, (1973) 4 SCC 225.

5. As held in Mercy vs Mankind (2015), compassion must inform the exercise of discretion.

6. The Delhi High Court took the same view in 2023:DHC:1234.
